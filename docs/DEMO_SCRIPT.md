# RELAY — 2:55 competition demo script

Final runtime: 2 minutes 46.59 seconds. The finished master was recorded from the production release at 1600 × 900 and exported at 1920 × 1080 with narration and burned-in captions. ChatGPT's in-app browser reported **27 tools live**.

## 0:00–0:15 — The problem

**On screen:** Fresh RELAY move plan, full first viewport.

**Narration:**

> Moving home means repeating one life event across council, utilities, post, insurance, and broadband. Agents can automate that work — but only if we can prove exactly what they may change, when they must stop, and what actually happened.

## 0:15–0:35 — One intent, explicit boundaries

**On screen:** Open **3-minute guide**, then close it and point to the move summary, protected-data note, and approval rules.

**Narration:**

> RELAY turns one intent into a scoped cross-service plan. Address changes are allowed. Any recurring price increase needs me. Employer details and marketing preferences are blocked before provider access. Everything stays inside this mission-control workspace — no copied agent prompt and no hidden tab choreography.

## 0:35–1:25 — Structured WebMCP coordination

**On screen:** Press **Review move plan**, then **Start move plan**. Let Civic, Northstar, Clearflow, Postway, and Haven complete. Briefly open **Activity** during the run, then close it.

**Narration:**

> Each fictional provider exposes focused WebMCP account, preview, and commit tools. RELAY calls them in order, checks the policy before each action, updates the real fictional account state, and creates an append-only receipt. The same callbacks drive both the visible workspace and the twenty-seven-tool WebMCP surface.

## 1:25–1:58 — Human approval becomes a real grant

**On screen:** Wavelength shows **Needs approval** at 83%. Press **Review this decision**. Hold on the exact approval facts, acknowledge, then approve.

**Narration:**

> RELAY stops when Wavelength raises the monthly price from thirty-six to forty-two pounds. My approval creates a ten-minute, single-use grant bound to this provider, this transfer, Fibre 300, forty-two pounds, the twelve-month contract, this postcode, and this move date. Changed terms need a new decision, and the grant can never be replayed.

## 1:58–2:18 — Proof of allowed action

**On screen:** Show 100% completion, the consumed grant, then open **Receipts** and select the Wavelength receipt.

**Narration:**

> The exact grant is consumed atomically, permanently closed, and linked to the sixth action receipt. The provider account, activity trace, grant, and receipt all agree.

## 2:18–2:43 — Proof of refusal

**On screen:** Open **Safety proof** and run **Provider changes the price**. Show the denial ID and **Blocked · no provider change**. If time allows, point to the other four challenges.

**Narration:**

> Winning demos should prove failure behavior too. These controlled challenges use the same enforcement as live commits. Here, a price change invalidates approval. RELAY also proves protected-data, expiry, replay, and out-of-order refusals — without mutating a provider or mixing refusal proof with successful receipts.

## 2:43–2:55 — Close

**On screen:** Return to the hero or briefly show `relay-architecture.svg`.

**Narration:**

> RELAY is a proposal for a safer agentic web: one human intent, many structured websites, exact delegation, human control, and verifiable proof. One intent. Many websites.

## Recording checklist

- Reset RELAY immediately before recording.
- Confirm the top-right chip says **27 tools live**, not **Demo mode**.
- Hide bookmarks, personal tabs, notifications, and unrelated browser chrome.
- Keep the pointer still while narrating; move only to the next action.
- Do not wait silently during execution — use that time for the WebMCP explanation.
- Keep the final edit under three minutes and export at 1080p or better.
- Add captions and verify every identifier remains readable after upload compression.
